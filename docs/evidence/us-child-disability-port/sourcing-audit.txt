Status: qualification port not landed. The existing administrative-only

Repository evidence
-------------------
ssi_take_up.py derives each prior from
    p = (T - F) / (C - F)
where T is the SSA target, F the eligible reporter floor, and C simulated
candidate capacity. Its delivered-weight artifact authenticates C and F;
it does not make the resulting rate independent of child qualification.
Using this prior while solving qualification produces
    F + p * (C - F) = T
for every feasible C. It cannot identify medical prevalence.

An AST-isolated execution of main's actual _band_prior (no mocked formula)
with T=1001922 and F=100000 demonstrated:
  C=2000000: p=.47469578947368424, expected recipients=1001922.
  C=4000000: p=.2312620512820513,  expected recipients=1001922.
Doubling eligible capacity still reproduces the target. Authenticating the
capacity artifact does not remedy this identification failure.

The old r5 draft's solver merely accepts a nonempty provenance dictionary;
the builder never invokes the solver. Its reporter mask also excludes
age-15--17 reporters from the under-18 reporter floor. Do not port these
behaviors. Current main already hashes staged-frame contents in target
checkpoint identity (materializer v13); a child stage must finish before
that digest is computed.

packages/microcosm-build/src/microcosm/build/us/take_up_contract.json,
definitions.provenance_rule, permits rates traceable to administrative
publications and excludes research-only estimates. No independent
administrative child SSI participation rate was identified in this audit.

Independent research evidence, requiring a disclosed exception
--------------------------------------------------------------
Levere and Wittenburg, Which Medicaid Recipients Might Be Eligible for SSI?,
National Tax Journal 79(2), June 2026, DOI 10.1086/739783:
https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/739783
Author September 2024 manuscript, p.19 and Appendix Table 4, pp.47--48:
https://hill1286.github.io/michaellevere/Levere%20Wittenburg%20(2024)%20-%20Which%20Medicaid%20Recipients%20Might%20be%20Eligible%20for%20SSI.pdf

The December 2019, ages-0--17 research estimate is
    (894687 + 262034) / (894687 + 262034 + 480514)
  = 1156721 / 1637235 = 0.7065088395984693, reported as 70.7%.
The numerator combines Medicaid SSI flags in 32 high-match states with SSA
counts elsewhere. The denominator estimates additional potential eligibility
from health-care utilization. Financial eligibility is assumed; people
outside Medicaid are omitted; predicted probabilities below 5% are excluded
in the preferred specification. The authors describe the estimate as an
upper bound. Their unrestricted alternative is 57.3%. This is not an SSA
administrative participation rate, and the study does not isolate the
federal-payment universe from state-supplement classifications.

SSA's December 2024 federal-payment child target is 1001922, comprising
893939 federal-only and 107983 federal-and-state recipients. The broader
1002887 count adds 965 state-only recipients:
https://www.ssa.gov/policy/docs/statcomps/ssi_monthly/2024-12/table01.html
Preserve the authenticated registry target; do not silently re-pin it.

Conditional arithmetic if the research exception is authorized
-------------------------------------------------------------
The study estimates overall participation r, not a nonreporter draw rate.
With all-under-18 eligible reporter floor F, target T, and fixed eligible
nonreporter age-15--17 capacity A:
    required qualifying eligible capacity C = T/r
    nonreporter prior p = (T-F)/(T/r-F)
    required under-15 nonreporter capacity = C-F-A
This p does not depend on the child threshold being solved.

Illustration only, T=1001922, F=100000, A=150000:
  r=.707: C=1417145.685997, p=.684754928470,
          required child capacity=1167145.685997.
  r=.573: C=1748554.973822, p=.547098528300,
          required child capacity=1498554.973822.
Both give F+p*(A+child capacity)=1001922. Neither illustration establishes
a severity threshold or a qualification prevalence on a population frame.

Diagnostic application after the qualification port is completed
---------------------------------------------------------------
No public child-stage functions are installed by this partial change.
Do not use the old draft as a drop-in diagnostic: it is unwired and its
criterion/anchor assumptions need repair.

The old draft names the intended interfaces:
  load_sipp_2023_child_disability_donor(path, expected_sha256, expected_size_bytes)
  with_us_child_disability_inputs(frame, seed, time_period, sipp_donor)
  child_ssi_medical_severity_scores(frame, sipp_donor, seed)
  with_us_child_ssi_financial_probe_criteria(frame, reporter_source_ids)
  with_us_child_ssi_medical_qualification(...)
These are reference interfaces on origin/ssi-453-child-disability-r5-wip,
not callable APIs on this branch.

A completed diagnostic must load an already released Frame with stable
source person identity, support-copy provenance, household/person membership,
age, sex, SSI_VAL reporter lineage, resolved person weights, general
disability and all baseline income/resource/parent-role engine inputs. Read
the pinned full SIPP donor locally through the verified stream, apply the
general layer, compute battery-only severity, then probe December uncapped_ssi
with all candidate children temporarily criteria-true. The probe needs
baseline financial inputs and real parent links; it must not use a reform.
Solve once with an independent rate and all-under-18 reporter floor, restore
persisted criteria, run take-up once at the same documented child prior,
and score the exported dataset. Compare every exported disability value with
the Frame. This is local diagnostic evidence, not release certification.

policyengine-us 2.2.1 does not deem a parent's resources to a child. Do not
compensate in the data layer. A solved severity threshold would depend on
the baseline engine's financial rules and must be re-solved when the engine
adds resource deeming. Parent income deeming must remain active in the probe.
