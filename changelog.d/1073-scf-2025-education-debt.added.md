The US build can impute federal student loan balances from the 2025 Survey of Consumer Finances (microcosm#1073, the EqRC student debt pilot). A new runtime module, `scf_education_debt`, writes four person inputs: `federal_student_loan_balance` and `federal_parent_plus_loan_balance` (policyengine-us#9721), and `is_income_based_repayment_new_borrower` and `federal_student_loan_graduate_share` (policyengine-us#9724).

Only education debt reads the 2025 SCF. Net worth, the liquid-asset leaves and auto loans stay on the 2022 SCF. The `scf_wealth` stage declares the two 2025 files (`scfp2025s.zip`, `scf2025s.zip`), pinned by zip and member SHA-256 against a fresh Federal Reserve download, as artifacts 3 and 4, and a `scf_2025` vintage points at them. Moving the whole stage to 2025 later changes pinned coordinates, not code: the 2025 summary extract carries every column the shared predictor construction reads.

The donor. Only loans flagged federal count. X7179, the total owed beyond the sixth loan, is spread over loans 1-6 in proportion to their balances, so it takes the family's federal share and holder split. Whose education decides the holder: the reference person's and the spouse's own loans go to `federal_student_loan_balance`; loans for a child go to the reference person's `federal_parent_plus_loan_balance`; codes 6 (multiple people), 7 (unrelated person) and -7 (other) go to the reference person's own balance. A person is an IBR new borrower when every federal loan they hold was borrowed in 2014 or later, and the graduate share weights each own loan's program by the amount borrowed.

The model. One weighted QRF draws each household's federal education debt. A second, fit on borrower families and conditioning on that total, draws the holder shares, graduate shares and new-borrower flags. Both condition on the reference person's age, sex and race, a spouse or partner being present, the children of the reference person or partner, and the wages and income less capital gains of the reference person, spouse or partner and their children. The CPS side codes each predictor the way the SCF defines it: SCF `MARRIED` counts cohabiting partners, SCF `KIDS` counts the partner's children, the reference person comes from the householder couple (the man, or the older partner), and income covers the primary economic unit rather than the whole household. The shared interest-dividend and Social Security-pension predictors are left out (`SCF_EDUCATION_DEBT_EXCLUDED_PREDICTORS`). The CPS host carries tax-record imputations of both, so at reference ages 25-34 it reports them for 42% and 18% of households against 16% and 6% of SCF families, and that shift was the main reason the first host fit drew too few borrowers. The reference person and the member its `A_SPOUSE` (else `PECOHAB`) pointer names receive the balances. A signal gate refuses negative, constant or incoherent columns and a borrower-household share outside 8-35%.

The fiscal-refresh release tool runs the stage only with `--scf-education-debt`, because the pinned policyengine-us does not define the four inputs yet. The four columns join `US_STORED_NON_VARIABLE_COLUMNS` with the pending-engine reason the parent ids carry, and leave it when the engine pin moves. They are not in the release input-coverage manifest, whose check requires every column to be an engine input. No release is certified with them.

Fit receipts are in `experiments/1073-scf-education-debt-receipts.md`, generated by `experiments/build_1073_scf_education_debt_receipt.py` on Modal through `experiments/modal_1073.py`, which clones a pushed commit, verifies every input's SHA-256 and records the run id in the receipt. The diagnosis behind the predictor and recipient choices is in `experiments/receipts/1073-predictor-shift-diagnosis.json` and `1073-recipient-definition-variants.json`.
