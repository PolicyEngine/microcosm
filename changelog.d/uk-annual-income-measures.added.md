The UK measure resolver now computes each HMRC income measure over a year of income as well as at the current rate: the 12 months before each FRS interview, applied to the engine's amounts at the calibration year, as a proxy for HMRC's tax year. Seven annual measures sit beside their current ones: `employment_income_annual`, `self_employment_income_after_loss_relief_annual`, `private_pension_income_annual`, `state_pension_annual`, `total_income_after_loss_relief_annual`, `income_tax_annual` and `tax_band_annual`. Each is its current measure computed on one more policyengine-uk simulation per resolver, whose inputs are each person's year from the `frs_annual_income_spells` descriptors, so the engine's own rules give the tax on that year's income.

policyengine-uk projects a dataset's inputs to its later years as it loads it, so the year's inputs are set at the measurement year: pay, profit, private pensions and contributory JSA, ESA and Incapacity Benefit at the engine's current amount times the annual share, plus the other spells carried from the dataset's year by the engine's own factor for that input, which must be one factor for every row. The State Pension takes the share of the fiscal year paid to someone who attains State Pension age within it (`months_since_state_pension_age`, measured to 6 October), and the earnings-tested carer benefits keep their baseline values. Pension contributions and salary sacrifice also keep their current values: for a part-year entrant they can be large against the year's pay, which lowers `income_tax_annual` a little. The simulation refuses if it does not read back an input it was given.

A frame without the descriptors refuses every annual measure, with one exception for the rule-1 scorer: the incumbent it scores against, built before the descriptors existed, reads its current values as its annual ones (a share of 1 and no other spells), and the score receipt names that route.
