The UK SPI income stage now floors drawn employment pay at the minimum wage for the twin's usual hours. Stage 1 draws annual tape pay on age, sex, region and the bridge predictors but not on hours, and the tape holds part-year workers, so a copy whose FRS twin reports usual hours in an employee job could draw a year's pay below what those hours earn at the minimum wage and fail the minimum-income tests of the 30 hours' childcare offer and Tax-Free Childcare more often than FRS rows. A new `floor_spi_employment_pay_at_minimum_wage` operation of `hmrc_spi_income_spine` lifts the drawn `hmrc_spi_pay` leaf to weekly usual hours (`hours_worked` read back at 365.25/7) times 52 weeks times policyengine-uk's `gov.hmrc.minimum_wage.non_apprentice` rate for the twin's age, read as the engine reads the build year (2024-25: £11.44 from 21, £8.60 from 18, £6.40 below). It runs after the stage-1 draw, the band-donor resample and the State Pension leaf guard, and before `employment_income` is derived from the leaves and stage 2 conditions on it, so the pension contributions, the HMRC aggregates and every later stage read the floored pay; the transport receipt keeps the unfloored draws' uniforms. It touches only drawn recipients other than reserved band carriers, whose twin has an employee main job, no trade and usual hours, and whose drawn pay is positive; self-employment and the other leaves keep their draws, and FRS rows are never modified. Tape PAY is taxable pay, net of net-pay pension contributions and salary sacrifice, while the minimum wage binds gross pay: PAY alone is compared with the gross minimum, since it is the leaf of the engine's gross `employment_income` input, so a twin at the minimum wage paying net-pay contributions can be lifted by up to them. The stage evidence gains an `employment_pay_floor` receipt with eligible and floored source households (rounded), weighted people, the weighted share floored and the weighted mean uplift, each "<10" on one to nine source households.
