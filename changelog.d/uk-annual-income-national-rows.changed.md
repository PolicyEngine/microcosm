The national HMRC income rows now measure each person's income over a year, as HMRC's statistics count income over the tax year. The year is a proxy for the tax year: the 12 months before each FRS interview (April 2024 to March 2025), applied to the engine's 2025 amounts and fitted to HMRC targets for calendar 2025 (the SPI 2023-24 rows uprated to it, and the Income Tax liabilities rows' calendar-2025 window). The 49 declarations of `hmrc_spi`, `hmrc_spi_region`, `hmrc_itl` and `hmrc_itl_marginal_rate` read the annual measures for their values, their total-income bands and filters, their taxpayer test and their rate band (`employment_income_annual`, `self_employment_income_after_loss_relief_annual`, `private_pension_income_annual`, `state_pension_annual`, `total_income_after_loss_relief_annual`, `income_tax_annual`, `tax_band_annual`), and `obr.income_tax` reads `income_tax_annual`. Dividends, property and savings income have no spells and stay as they are. Target ids, facts, values and metric names do not change: only the person-side measure each row adds up moves, so a recent leaver whose year of income makes them liable to income tax counts as a taxpayer, and a part-year earner sits in the band of what they received. Poverty, benefits and the engine's published tax keep reading current income. Reform costings on files built from this code are lower. These figures come from a pre-Budget arm at `e399d21ef`, signed with a throwaway key and not attested, against #1338's certified build at `829c1bc26`, both on policyengine-uk 2.147.2; the first certified build from main confirms them, and this note changes with it if they move materially. In 2025 the engine's income tax on current income falls 1.0% (£324.7bn to £321.5bn; now below OBR's receipts on this build, since the calibration fits OBR's total on the year's income, and part-year entrants pull annual income below current income while leavers push it above), and static costings of threshold changes fall 1.7% to 1.9% (a £1,000 rise in the personal allowance from £8.98bn to £8.81bn, and 1p on the rUK basic rate from £6.62bn to £6.51bn). These are full-year liabilities of every UK taxpayer, which HMRC's 2025-26 ready reckoner (January 2025, with behaviour) is not, for two reasons. Its first-year column counts Self Assessment tax when it is paid, so it holds little of a change's Self Assessment effect: fitted to HMRC's own later columns, a full year is 9% to 11% higher, about £8.45bn for £1,000 on the allowance (from £0.98bn for 1% and £9.65bn for 10%) and £7.28bn for 1p (from £6.55bn). And it is the UK government's exchequer impact, which leaves out the devolved Scottish and Welsh elements and adds the change in their block grants. On that scope, in calendar 2025, the allowance rise costs £8.54bn on this arm against £8.71bn on #1338's build, moving from 3.1% to 1.1% above HMRC's full year, and 1p, with the rUK property income that the engine's main-rate parameter does not reach, raises £7.07bn against £7.18bn, moving from 1.3% to 2.9% below. Moved to 2025-26, this arm costs about 1% more on both, so like for like the allowance sits about 1.9% above HMRC and 1p about 1.9% below (microcosm#1370). The CGT taxable-income bands and the salary-sacrifice reliefs stay on current income.
