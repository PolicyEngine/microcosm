The national HMRC income rows now measure each person's income over a year, as HMRC's statistics count income over the tax year. The year is a proxy for the tax year: the 12 months before each FRS interview (April 2024 to March 2025), applied to the engine's 2025 amounts and fitted to HMRC targets for calendar 2025 (the SPI 2023-24 rows uprated to it, and the Income Tax liabilities rows' calendar-2025 window). The 49 declarations of `hmrc_spi`, `hmrc_spi_region`, `hmrc_itl` and `hmrc_itl_marginal_rate` read the annual measures for their values, their total-income bands and filters, their taxpayer test and their rate band (`employment_income_annual`, `self_employment_income_after_loss_relief_annual`, `private_pension_income_annual`, `state_pension_annual`, `total_income_after_loss_relief_annual`, `income_tax_annual`, `tax_band_annual`), and `obr.income_tax` reads `income_tax_annual`. Dividends, property and savings income have no spells and stay as they are. Target ids, facts, values and metric names do not change: only the person-side measure each row adds up moves, so a recent leaver whose year of income makes them liable to income tax counts as a taxpayer, and a part-year earner sits in the band of what they received. Poverty, benefits and the engine's published tax keep reading current income. Reform costings on files built from this code are lower. These figures come from a pre-Budget arm at `e399d21ef`, signed with a throwaway key and not attested, against #1338's certified build at `829c1bc26`, both on policyengine-uk 2.147.2; the first certified build from main confirms them, and this note changes with it if they move materially. In 2025 the engine's income tax on current income falls 1.0% (£324.7bn to £321.5bn; now below OBR's receipts on this build, since the calibration fits OBR's total on the year's income, and part-year entrants pull annual income below current income while leavers push it above), and static costings of threshold changes fall 1.7% to 1.9% (a £1,000 rise in the personal allowance from £8.98bn to £8.81bn, and 1p on the basic rate from £6.62bn to £6.51bn), closer to HMRC's 2025-26 ready reckoner (January 2025, which includes behaviour: £6.55bn for 1p, and £0.78bn for a £100 rise and £9.65bn for a 10% rise, which put £1,000 near £7.7bn to £7.8bn). The CGT taxable-income bands and the salary-sacrifice reliefs stay on current income.
