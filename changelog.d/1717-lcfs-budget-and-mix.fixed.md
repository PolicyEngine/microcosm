The LCFS consumption stage's imputed spending ran a quarter above its donor in the bottom income decile (microcosm#1717). Two fixes.

The donor's income predictors carry the frame's concepts. `employment_income` is `p008p`, normal gross wage or salary on the 13-week rule, where it was `b303p`, the pay last time paid. `self_employment_income` sums `p047p` and `p037p`, income from main and from subsidiary self-employment, where it was `b3262p`, the first subsidiary job's profit alone (LCF 2024-25 User Guide Volume F). The frame's predictors are gross pay and self-employment profit.

The spending is no longer drawn target by target, each target conditioned on the earlier random draws, which compounded the forests' pull to the middle at the bottom of the income range. The new `draw_budget_and_mix` operation draws each household's budget, total spending over the twelve COICOP divisions, from its neighbourhood in a forest fitted to the donors' log total spending, read between neighbouring donors' totals. Its mix comes from one donor drawn from a second forest's neighbourhood, fitted to the division shares with the predictors and log total spending as features. The household takes that donor's spending scaled to its budget: the divisions sum to the budget, each fuel, fare and energy part keeps the donor's share of its division, and the excise shares are the donor's own. The scaled basket stays inside the donor's range on every spend target the support clip bounds, so the clip changes nothing: a household whose donor's basket, scaled to its budget, would leave that range keeps its budget and takes a donor drawn afresh from the neighbours whose basket fits, and one none of whose neighbours fits takes the budget nearest its own that its donor's basket allows. Every draw reads uniforms keyed on the household id. The general tools are `forest_neighbourhoods`, `keyed_neighbour_donors` and `keyed_neighbour_quantiles` in `microcosm.fit.donors`; the `microcosm.fit` package itself is unchanged.

On the LCFS 2024-25 diaries, fitted on half the households to impute the other half over 12 splits, total spending in the bottom decile comes out at 0.97 of actual, and every other decile within 5%. The spread of spending among similar households is 1.05 of actual, and the correlations between divisions sit at their sampling floor. On the stage's own input, 0.5% of frame households share an exact basket with another: mixes repeat, at 34,953 frame households on 4,995 donors, and budgets do not.

A `decile_fit` receipt sets the frame's spending beside the donor's by income decile on every build. Each survey is ranked on its own equivalised predictor income, and the receipt gives aggregates with support bands, withholding any decile on fewer than three source households. It gives each figure against the donor as surveyed and against the donor reweighted, within each decile, to the frame's household mix of adults, children, cars and tenure. In that reweighting a category with fewer than three donor households joins another, a category the frame lacks gets no weight, and no household's weight moves more than five times either way. The report-only gate `uk_stage_lcfs_consumption_decile_fit` warns when a decile 1 to 9 leaves the reweighted donor's shape, its ratio over the pooled ratio of deciles 4 to 7, by more than 10%, so a difference between the two surveys' household mixes does not set it off. Given each household's mix donor, the receipt also gives the effective number of donors behind the frame's mixes in each decile and in each region's deciles, the number of distinct mixes to expect when cutting spending by such a group. A `budget_and_mix` receipt records the draw's range step on every build: the support band and shares of the households given a new donor and of those moved to another budget. The receipt's official reference is ONS Family Spending, financial year ending 2025 (workbook 1, Table 3.1E, by equivalised disposable income decile on the updated OECD-modified scale), vendored from the re-pinned Chronicle feed as `ons_family_spending_income_decile_facts.json`. For each decile, in total and by division, it sets the published weekly mean and its shape beside the frame's and the donor's own profiles, each decile's mean over its deciles 4 to 7.

A household with a negative income, a self-employment loss say, draws like any other: the forests read incomes as values, so it sorts with the donors at the bottom of every split on income.
