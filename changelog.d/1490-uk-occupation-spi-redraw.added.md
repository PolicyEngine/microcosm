A new UK spine stage, `spi_employment_shell`, redraws the SOC 2020 major group of the SPI-drawn rows from their own incomes (microcosm#1490). The SPI support copies and income band donors are whole FRS households, so a drawn row kept its twin's occupation, industry and sector, all chosen with the twin's pay. The stage trains on the FRS rows' adults with an occupation and conditions on age, sex, region, employment-status group and employment, self-employment, investment and private pension income. Its classifier is microcosm-fit's new country-agnostic `CategoricalClassifier`: the equal blend of a regularised gradient-boosted classifier and a spline multinomial logit, the learner that held out best on FRS 2024-25. Each drawn row then copies the SIC division and sector of an FRS adult with the drawn group, the same status group and the nearest age and incomes, so occupation and industry stay a pair that occurs. A drawn row whose twin had no occupation and that drew no pay or profit keeps NONE, and FRS rows and undrawn copies are untouched.

The receipt records held-out evidence on five household-grouped folds (log loss against the constant and each learner, the calibration slope, and occupation-share gaps within pay bands and status groups), and a stage-health gate, `uk_stage_spi_employment_shell_fit`, holds them to declared bounds. Because the stage rewrites `employment_sector`, it moves the `ons.public_sector_employment` calibration target's measured value.
