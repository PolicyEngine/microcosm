The UK and US hold-outs share their last two steps (microcosm#1759). `microcosm.build.holdout` gains `HoldoutSplit` (a split's training rows and held rows), `fold_splits` (the UK's rotated folds as splits) and `held_estimates` (the held rows' estimates from the target matrix and the solved weights, in float64). The UK rowwise hold-out (`uk_runtime.local_rowwise.rotated_uk_local_holdout`) and the US local hold-out (`microcosm.build.target_holdouts`, through the new `role_split`) both use them; the two draws stay apart on purpose (rotated folds over UK local target rows, hashed blocks over US units). No hold-out result changes: `test_holdout.py` holds the new functions to the replaced UK and US expressions byte for byte on float64 matrices, which is what both builds pass (a float32 matrix with float32 weights is widened before the product, where the replaced UK expression multiplied in float32), and the US hold-out tests compare with verbatim copies of the old tool code. The UK hold-out node now also hashes `microcosm.build.holdout`, which it ran without hashing, and the modules it reaches when it rebuilds the problem inputs: the declared level rule (`microcosm.calibrate.target_sets`, `uk_runtime.target_sets`) and the review core (`microcosm.build.declared_registers`). `test_uk_graph_terminal.py` pins these entries and the gate kernels' review-core entry. The UK hold-out keeps its constants (5 folds, seed 20260529, in `uk_runtime/local_rowwise.py`); a stricter UK hold-out would start there.
