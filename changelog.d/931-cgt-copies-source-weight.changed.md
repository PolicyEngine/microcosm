The CGT support split (`cgt_support_split`) counts each selected household's copies on its weight scaled to its source, `ceil(K × w / 60)`, where `K` is the frame's geographic clone count (#931). A geographic clone carries 1/K of its source household's weight, so a selected clone whose weight is an exact 1/K replica of its source's (which the per-clone strata of the later #931 commits give) gets the count its source gets on a one-clone spine, and each copy weighs at most 60 / K. The stage reads `K` from the frame: one more than the largest `household_clone_index`, or 1 where the column is absent, as on every national spine today. It refuses, by count, indices that are not exactly 0 to K − 1. The selection walk still accumulates each row's own weight, so selection nests only approximately: the household at which a band's walk reaches its support mass, and households tied on wealth, can be selected in some clones and not others, lowest household id first. For K > 1 the quotient is snapped to the nearest integer when it is within K ulps of it, so a weight that is an exact multiple of 60 keeps its count at any K; at K = 7 the rounding of the clone weight would otherwise add a copy for some exact multiples. The snap gives up the converse case: a quotient within K ulps above an integer counts one copy fewer than at K = 1. At K = 1 nothing is snapped, and the count is `ceil(w / 60)` bit for bit, as before.

- **Receipt and gate.** The stage receipt's parameters gain `clone_count`, and nothing else is new. The stage-health gate compares K times the heaviest copy, and K times the heaviest selected household, with the maximum copy weight, so a receipt at K > 1 no longer fails falsely. At K = 1 both checks and their messages are unchanged. A receipt without a positive integer `clone_count` is refused. The identity tool's recompute (`tools/verify_uk_identity_stability.py`) reads K from the pre-split table, as the stage reads it from its input, and its receipt keys are unchanged. At every K its count check accepts a family's stored count within the family weights' relative tolerance of 1e-9, because folding the family's copies back re-sums them, which can land ulps from the family's weight: at K = 1 for a weight a few ulps above a multiple of 60, or where the stage's exact-total correction fell on a copy, and at K > 1 by more than the snap absorbs. So for a family whose weight lies within 1e-9 of a multiple of 60, the check accepts n or n + 1 copies. A stored count moved there on its own is still reported, by the flag and family-weight checks. A family the stage split consistently into one copy more there, every member at w / (n + 1), goes unreported at every K. At K = 1 the exact check used to report it. The receipt's tolerance policy says so.
- **Declarations.** The `copy_rule` text in the code dictionary, `sources.yaml` and `source_stages.json` now reads "copies = ceil(geographic clone count x household weight / maximum_copy_weight), the quotient snapped to the nearest integer within clone-count ulps when the clone count exceeds one; every copy carries weight / copies, except where none of the 64 heaviest households left whole can carry the exact-total correction, when the shared fallback can put it on a root or copy, off weight / copies by about one ulp of the total weight". The high-value home support split's (`high_value_home_support`) reads "every selected household becomes copies rows at weight / copies", followed by the same exception. Both stages' notes say where the correction lands. The CGT stage notes give the cap as 60 divided by the geographic clone count before the incidence clone, and name the incidence clone stage (`cgt_incidence_clone`) where they mean it.
- **Exact total.** The exact-total correction lands on a household the split left whole: a positive-weight household it did not divide, unselected or selected at one copy, whose weight the split left as it was. The stage tries the 64 heaviest of them (`CGT_SUPPORT_CORRECTION_CANDIDATES`), heaviest first with tied weights in row order, and the first that can carry the correction takes it, so every root and copy weighs bitwise w / n. A selected household left at one copy carries it only where its corrected weight leaves the walk's selection and its one copy as they were, so the rule rerun on the stage's output, as the identity tool reruns it, divides the same households; on the synthetic probe frames, 11 corrections would otherwise have lifted a one-copy household to a weight that counts two copies, and the identity tool's recompute would have found a family the stage never made. Only where none of them can does spi_support's shared fallback place it, unchanged, and that row can be a root or a copy. The row is then off w / n by about one ulp of the frame's total weight: thousands of ulps of its own weight, a relative error below 1e-12 on the synthetic frames. On the synthetic nesting scenario at K = 7 and 13 every whole household sits in the half of NumPy's pairwise sum whose other half carries exactly half an ulp of the total, so no change to its weight rounds the total onto the target, and the fallback still puts the correction on a copy. Before, the stage tried only the heaviest unselected household. That is still the first candidate wherever no selected household left at one copy weighs as much, which needs every unselected household to weigh at most 60 / K. The high-value home support split (stage 26) corrects through the same helper and cap. Every household it selects becomes six rows, so its whole households are its unselected positive ones, and its first candidate is always the household it corrected before. The cap bounds the cost where no whole household can carry the correction: on a synthetic vector of 15 × 78,188 rows, the size of a K = 15 spine, 64 such households and the fallback take about 0.4 s, against 0.02 s for the one candidate before.
