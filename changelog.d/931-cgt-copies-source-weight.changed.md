The CGT support split (`cgt_support_split`) counts each selected household's copies on its weight scaled to its source, `ceil(K × w / 60)`, where `K` is the frame's geographic clone count (#931). A geographic clone carries 1/K of its source household's weight, so a selected clone whose weight is an exact 1/K replica of its source's (which the per-clone strata of the later #931 commits give) gets the count its source gets on a one-clone spine, and each copy weighs at most 60 / K. The stage reads `K` from the frame: one more than the largest `household_clone_index`, or 1 where the column is absent, as on every national spine today. It refuses, by count, indices that are not exactly 0 to K − 1. The selection walk still accumulates each row's own weight, so selection nests only approximately: the household at which a band's walk reaches its support mass, and households tied on wealth, can be selected in some clones and not others, lowest household id first. For K > 1 the quotient is snapped to the nearest integer when it is within K ulps of it, so a weight that is an exact multiple of 60 keeps its count at any K; at K = 7 the rounding of the clone weight would otherwise add a copy for some exact multiples. The snap gives up the converse case: a quotient within K ulps above an integer counts one copy fewer than at K = 1. At K = 1 nothing is snapped, and the count is `ceil(w / 60)` bit for bit, as before.

- **Receipt and gate.** The stage receipt's parameters gain `clone_count`, and nothing else is new. The stage-health gate compares K times the heaviest copy, and K times the heaviest selected household, with the maximum copy weight, so a receipt at K > 1 no longer fails falsely. At K = 1 both checks and their messages are unchanged. A receipt without a positive integer `clone_count` is refused. The identity tool's recompute (`tools/verify_uk_identity_stability.py`) reads K from the pre-split table, as the stage reads it from its input, and its receipt keys are unchanged. At every K its count check accepts a family's stored count within the family weights' relative tolerance of 1e-9, because folding the family's copies back re-sums them, which can land ulps from the family's weight: at K = 1 for a weight a few ulps above a multiple of 60, or where the stage's exact-total correction fell on a copy, and at K > 1 by more than the snap absorbs. On an exact multiple of 60 the check therefore accepts n or n + 1 copies, and a count moved there is reported by the flag and family-weight checks instead. The receipt's tolerance policy says so.
- **Declarations.** The `copy_rule` text in the code dictionary, `sources.yaml` and `source_stages.json` now reads "copies = ceil(geographic clone count x household weight / maximum_copy_weight), the quotient snapped to the nearest integer within clone-count ulps when the clone count exceeds one; every copy carries weight / copies". The stage notes give the cap as 60 divided by the geographic clone count before the incidence clone, and name the incidence clone stage (`cgt_incidence_clone`) where they mean it.
- **Exact total.** The exact-total correction is unchanged. It lands on the heaviest unselected household, so every copy weighs bitwise w / n, except where that household cannot carry it: spi_support's shared fallback then places it, and that row can be a root or a copy, a few ulps off w / n.
- **What moves.** No value moves at K = 1. On synthetic frames, the stage's output and the identity tool's receipt are unchanged: ids, copy counts, weights bit for bit, mass records and frame content identity. At K = 1 the identity tool no longer reports a count problem for a family that folds back within the tolerance of its stored count. The stage evidence gains `clone_count`. `cgt_support_split`'s stage contract moves with its texts. Its implementation hash moves, and so does `high_value_home_support`'s, which binds `cgt_support`. The spine gates hash `stage_health` and the country spec, both of which moved, so their keys move, and with them every spine stage after the assembled gate. The coverage manifest's source digests and the H2 fixture's declarations and contract hash are regenerated.
