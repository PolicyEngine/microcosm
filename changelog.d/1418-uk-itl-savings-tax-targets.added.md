Three UK national calibration rows, `hmrc.itl.savings_tax_at_{basic,higher,additional}_rate`, bind the Income Tax on savings at each rate that Table 2.6 of HMRC's Income Tax liabilities statistics (July 2026) projects for all payers, at the calendar-2025 window of the 2024-25 and 2025-26 projections (microcosm#1418). They are the base the Budget's savings income rates are costed on (Personal Tax Model on SPI 2023-24: taxable savings income above the Personal Allowance, the Personal Savings Allowance and the starting rate, ISAs excluded). Each row measures the rate times the engine's savings income at that rate on each person's year, through three new annual measures (`basic_rate_savings_income_annual`, `higher_rate_savings_income_annual`, `add_rate_savings_income_annual`); a value expression may now scale a name by a positive constant for that. SPI Table 3.7's bank and building society interest rows stay. Known gap, fixed in PR C: HMRC's savings tax grows x1.52 from 2023-24 to the calendar-2025 window while the SPI Table 3.7 interest rows grow x1.192, so the national solve meets the three rows mostly through `other_investment_income`, the one savings column no target binds: on the first cut built with them it is GBP 9.5bn in 2024 against GBP 3.5bn before, above SPI 2023-24 Table 3.7's GBP 5.85bn of other income.
