The UK LCFS stage splits the diary's fuel, light and power (p537) into electricity and gas by its labelled parts, from the LCF User Guide's derived-variable labels and P537 definition (microcosm#1164). The derivation it replaces, ported from the incumbent uk-data pipeline, read b226 as electricity paid by direct debit and b489 and b490 as prepayment-meter payments. The labels make b226 the gas component of a combined utilities bill (b227 is the electricity one) and b489 and b490 the electricity and gas account payments less rebates (b175 less b178, b170 less b173), so it gave combined-bill households, most of the donor, their gas as electricity; took gas away from electricity for households with two accounts; split an electricity account between the fuels; and folded oil and solid fuels into the drawn energy. Each fuel is now its account payment less its rebate (b489, b490 as the tab carries them; the stage refuses a donor where either equals its payment less its rebate for under 95 % of that fuel's account payers, which pins the labels and the rebate's sign), its prepayment key or meter payment, its part of a combined bill or meter payment, and the diary's second-dwelling account and slot-meter lines. A combined payment left unsplit, net of the combined bill's rebate (b174), goes by the household's own electricity share where it pays for both fuels and by the donor's mean share of dual-fuel households otherwise, since a combined payment means it pays for both; a donor with no dual-fuel household fails rather than taking a fixed share. Oil, bottled gas, solid fuels and heat stay in the housing total. A `donor_energy_split` receipt records the donor's electricity, gas and other fuels against p537, what reading refunds as zero adds, the weighted share of donors whose unclipped parts differ from their p537, and the account identity shares. Negative amounts (refunds) are read as zero. The NEED rake and the DESNZ level still set each fuel's level, but which households carry electricity and which gas now follows what they reported.
