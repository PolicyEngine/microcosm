The ONS Annual Population Survey's people in employment by SOC 2020 occupation bind as calibration targets (microcosm#1490). The nine major groups each bind over the UK's twelve region-tier areas (108 references, María's ruling of 4 November 2026), and the 26 sub-major groups bind for men and for women at UK level (52 references, her ruling of 5 November 2026). They read Nomis APS218 for January to December 2025, all employment types, through PolicyEngine/chronicle#411. Each counts the people whose `employment_status` is full-time or part-time employed or self-employed and whose `soc2020_major_group`, or `soc2020_sub_major_group` and `gender`, match the row. Out-of-work adults keep their last job's group and are not counted, and the APS also counts unpaid family workers. Before these rows, nothing in the fit fixed how employment splits across occupations, which PolicyEngine/uk-ai-study#16 reads. With only the major groups bound, the sub-major draw left most sub-major groups, and their mix of men and women, outside the APS's confidence intervals on the calibrated frame. Regional sub-major cells stay unbound: their intervals are wide, and in cross-validation binding them did not improve the cells held out.
