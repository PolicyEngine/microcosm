The local line computes each metric from its declaration. `compute_household_metrics` reads every constituency, local-authority and BRMA metric's `policyengine` binding in `uk_population_targets.json` through the national rows' binding interpreter (`prepared_binding_values`), on columns `UKMeasureResolver` computes, and sums person and benefit-unit bindings onto their household with the engine's `map_result`. A rebind therefore moves the national and local lines together, and a local declaration that carries a key the local line does not honour (a measurement period, a band, a mapping other than to the household) refuses. Ten local declarations change so they say what the code computed: the four UC child bands count UC benefit units by `uc_calibration_child_count`, the FRS-role composition the national UC-by-children rows read; the private rents read `benunit_rent` summed to the household; the high-value homes and the gas-connected households read resolver measures that apply the stored period and price basis (`owner_occupied_home_over_gbp_*`, and the new `domestic_gas_connected` beside `domestic_electricity_kwh` and `domestic_gas_kwh`). The interpreter gains `in` lists, lower-inclusive ranges and subtraction in value expressions, and refuses a predicate that declares `input_period` or `price_basis`, which it cannot honour. On a national frame, the 59 local declarations' 43 metrics (61 columns over the three grains) are identical to the old code's, bit for bit, except `ons/equiv_housing_costs`, which no active target reads: it differs by float32 rounding because the difference is now taken in float64. The local references change only the entity they record: `benunit` for the UC child bands, `household` for the private rents. The incumbent extractor reads the incumbent file through one resolver; an incumbent without FRS benefit-unit roles, such as the enhanced FRS, cannot measure the UC child bands, which the national scorer's signed incumbent-unresolvable register already records for `uc_calibration_child_count`.
