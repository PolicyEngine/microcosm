A build on a busy host no longer loses its hosted telemetry when another build holds the shared local spool (`~/.cache/microcosm/telemetry/events.sqlite3`) while its emitter service starts. Before, the service's registration gave up after SQLite's 5 s busy wait, the service exited, and a SQLAlchemy traceback landed on the build's stderr. Now the build passes the time it will stop waiting (`--ready-deadline`). The service waits for the lock until 0.5 s before then: each SQLite wait ends by that point and lasts at most 5 s, a failed attempt is retried with jittered backoff, and the whole wait is capped at 60 s. Without a deadline it makes one 5 s attempt, as before. If the spool is still locked, it prints one warning line and exits with status 75 before the build gives up. Any other startup failure is also one line: status 2 for invalid arguments, 1 otherwise. SIGINT ends the service quietly, as SIGTERM does.

Opening a spool already at the current schema takes no write lock. Retention pruning moved from spool open and every append to the delivery worker. It deletes in batches of 500 rows, each in its own short transaction, pauses 25 ms between batches so other builds' writers get the lock, and works at most 1 s per worker tick. A prune with nothing to remove only reads. Migrations run in one `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` transaction. Concurrent first opens of a new spool no longer fail with "table already exists"; about half of eight simultaneous opens did before. An interrupted migration leaves no partial schema, and a spool stamped by a migration this checkout does not know is refused without taking the lock.

The delivery worker skips a failed step for one tick instead of dying. Heartbeats, delivery, and the check that records a killed build keep running. Lock contention is retried silently, and any other error prints one line per error type. An unexpected delivery error backs off like a collector failure. Warnings never raise on a closed stderr, and the service stops serving if its worker ever dies. A build that closes and exits while a worker step is still running is no longer recorded as an unexpected exit after its completion.
