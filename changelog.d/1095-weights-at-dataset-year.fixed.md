A UK release writes its household weights at the dataset's own year (microcosm#796 A19, microcosm#1095; uk-data#538 is the incumbent's side of the same mechanism). policyengine-uk projects a single-year dataset forward: from the dataset's year it multiplies `household_weight` by one plus the ONS population growth of each later year. Weights solved at 2025 and stored on the 2024-25 dataset therefore read 0.72% high at 2025, on every weighted count and total. The release write now divides the solved weights by the engine's own factor (`uk_runtime.weight_year`), so the engine returns the solved weights at the solve year. The division is the last record of the file's mass log, and the national and local readbacks expect exactly it. The solve, its gates, its diagnostics and the engine scratch file keep the solved weights. The growth is vendored in a new `dataset_projection` block of `uk/hmrc_uprating_engine_pins.json` and a test holds it against the installed engine, with a second test that `household_weight` is the only weight the engine projects and a third that loads a written file and reads the solved weights back at 2025. Readers of a written file follow the same projection: the rule-1 scorer reads each side with the weights the engine gives it at the scored year and its score block gains `weight_year`, so scores are not comparable with earlier ones; the release-cut certifier runs its gates on the solved weights and refuses a file divided for another year; `tools/evaluate_uk_incumbent_surface.py` reads the candidate the same way; and the assembler's build manifest says which year the weights NPZ stands at (it pairs with the H5 row by row, so it carries the file's own weights).
