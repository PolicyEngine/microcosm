A new last spine stage, `trading_allowance_receipts`, stores the self-employed's gross receipts (`self_employment_gross_receipts`), so policyengine-uk applies the trading allowance against receipts as the law does (#1428; María's choice of 2026-10-31). Neither the FRS nor the SPI tape records turnover. Without receipts the engine relieved profits up to the allowance in full and nothing above it, so a rise in the allowance missed its main base: traders who already claim the £1,000 allowance against larger receipts. Under current law a claimant's receipts are their profit plus £1,000 exactly. Each trader with a positive profit is flagged as a claimant with probability 0.72 for profits up to £4,500, 0.14 from £4,500 to £9,000 and none above, drawn on their own `person_id`. A claimant's receipts are their profit plus £1,000; any other trader's are their profit plus £3,000, expenses at the raised allowance, since the costing books traders who switch to the allowance as behaviour. The rates are calibrated to HMRC's policy paper (about 400,000 taxpayers with a lower bill, by about £380 on average), measured on policyengine-uk for the national from 9f70c71e7, until HMRC publishes claimant counts; they drift with each national, so #1439 re-measures them after each build. Every trader with a positive profit is treated as filing, as the Table 3.10 counts the calibration binds are of filers, so a profit of £1,000 or less is now taxed where the engine used to relieve it in full. Two limits remain until policyengine-uk#2765 lets the stage store the claim status itself (#1439). A claimant's £1,000 grows with the mixed income index while the allowance is nominal, so the raise relieves £1,932 more in 2027-28, falling to £1,758 by 2031-32. The other traders' stored £3,000 grows the same way, so allowances above £3,000 are mis-scored. `self_employment_gross_receipts` leaves the register of engine inputs left at their default and joins the export surface's allowed columns.
