A new last spine stage, `trading_allowance_receipts`, stores the self-employed's gross receipts (`self_employment_gross_receipts`), so policyengine-uk applies the trading allowance against receipts as the law does (#1428; María's choice of 2026-10-31). Neither the FRS nor the SPI tape records turnover. Without receipts the engine relieved profits up to the allowance in full and nothing above it, so a rise in the allowance missed its main base: traders who already claim the £1,000 allowance against larger receipts. Under current law a claimant's receipts are their profit plus £1,000 exactly. Each trader with a positive profit is flagged as a claimant with probability 0.64 for profits up to £4,500, 0.32 from £4,500 to £9,000 and none above, drawn on their own `person_id`. A claimant's receipts are their profit plus £1,000; any other trader's are their profit plus £3,000, expenses at the raised allowance, since the costing books traders who switch to the allowance as behaviour. The rates are calibrated to HMRC's policy paper (about 400,000 taxpayers with a lower bill, by about £380 on average) until HMRC publishes claimant counts. Every trader with a positive profit is treated as filing, as the Table 3.10 counts the calibration binds are of filers, so a profit of £1,000 or less is now taxed where the engine used to relieve it in full. `self_employment_gross_receipts` leaves the register of engine inputs left at their default and joins the export surface's allowed columns.
