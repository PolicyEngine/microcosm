Add `tools/draft_uk_dense_signoff.py`, which drafts the gate-only sign-off between the two dense UK builds (microcosm#1301). From build 1's run directory it reads the full gate report, the signed local gate report, the target and area-support tables and the Logbook row, checks them against one another and against the checkout's `gates.json`, and drafts `release_lines: ["local"]` entries for every failing national target-fit row, local target-fit cell and area below the support floor. It also drafts the removal of every entry build 2 would fail and the test pins those registers move, as a `git apply` patch with a PR body of counts, gate ids and policy dates. A national target-fit entry that applies on every line is never removed or replaced, since the national line may still stand on it: when build 2 would fail it on the local line it is rescoped to `release_lines: ["national"]`, otherwise unchanged. An expired one also goes to review for the national line to renew or retire, and the draft is unsignable until then if keeping it breaks the national graph test's review invariant; a row build 1 still breaches under such an entry needs a hand sign-off, since a register key holds one entry. Any other release-blocking failure makes the build unsignable, and so does an area exclusion the local reference generator's own deferrals keep, when build 2 would fail it. The receipt and the PR body give the assembly deadline: the earliest expiry among the measure exclusions build 1's solve ran under (from its candidate manifest, or from the target compilation's surface when its gates stopped it before the export), the local line's degenerate-register entries and every gate-only entry build 2's gates hold to its window after the sign-off (those on the local line, every `bound_despite_support_floor` family whatever its line, and the local line's input-mass entries, which the tool reads but never drafts). Build 2's gate-clock range ends there and starts at the latest of build 1's clock and those entries' approvals. A gate clock past the solve-scoped expiries is refused, and an input-mass entry out of its window at the gate clock makes the draft unsignable until it is renewed by hand. Reasons are templated from the key, the gate's bound or the floors the area missed, the build-1 attempt id and the full gate report's sha256. `--approved-by` must be a GitHub login that starts with a letter and holds at most one run of digits; the reviewer's `--class-note`, `--area-name` and `--adjudication` text may carry digits only in `microcosm#N`, `chronicle#N`, `uk-data#N` or `PolicyEngine/<repo>#N` references (optionally anchored `#issuecomment-N`), GitHub URLs of a PolicyEngine issue or pull request and ISO dates of real days in 2000–2099, never in a bare `#N`, and no percent sign in any form; and the drafted entries then get the release contract's disclosure walk, where a finding refuses the draft, though the walk only warns on a release directory; on text the walk catches only an area's support worded with its measured value, and none of these checks reads a figure written in words. Per-row errors, per-area support figures and the local registers' stale-risk and near-bound counts go only to a private review file (the local counts also to the tool's standard output); the PR body and the receipt carry the national register's counts alone. `--out` must lie outside every git work tree, `.gitignore` covers `PRIVATE_review.csv` and `signoff-draft/`, and the support table's maximum weight is never read. `--verify` checks that build 2 stood on exactly the committed entries, that no release-blocking gate failed and that its signed local report passes the dense contract, and, with `--draft-receipt`, that the sign-off committed what was drafted and changed each rescoped entry in its release lines only; it warns when the disclosure walk finds a per-area support figure in that report, fails once the assembly deadline has passed, warns from two days before it, and reports whether build 2 reused build 1's solve. The three fixed-clock target-fit tests in `test_uk_terminal_gates.py` now read the register as the national line does, so local-line sign-offs approved after their clock leave them unchanged.
