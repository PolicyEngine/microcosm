The UK stage kernels' `_minimal_frame` and the UK `context_frame` copy each of a kernel's input tables once when they build its `Frame`. The graph executor hands kernels tables that hold one pandas block per column, and `copy(deep=True)` of such a table copies every column, stacks the copies of each NumPy dtype into a new block and reorders it, and the `Frame` constructor then copied the result again. The new `microcosm.frame.table_copies.copy_table` copies each column straight into its place in the same block layout, and `Frame.adopt` keeps the copies. Values, dtypes, blocks, column offsets, index, `attrs`, strata, weights and metadata are the same as before, so no data output changes; the UK kernels' implementation hashes move because their modules changed, and none of them is pinned.

Measured with `tracemalloc` from the executor's projection to the kernel's `Frame`, through both functions, on an invented UK population of 1,000,000 persons (20 float64 and 2 int64 columns read, 167.8 MiB): the peak goes from 3.03 to 1.49 times the columns read, and what stays held is 1.30 times before and after (it includes the id and membership columns, the benefit unit and household tables and the strata).

A write into the kernel's `Frame` through its index or through a categorical column's or the strata's categories no longer reaches the context or the stored population. pandas' copy views the index, and `np.add.at` writes even into the executor's read-only view of it; it shares a categorical's categories; and a `RangeIndex`, even deep-copied, shares the cache holding its materialized values, which `np.asarray` returns writable. The copy therefore copies the index and column labels (one array per table whose index is not a `RangeIndex`), rebuilds every `RangeIndex`, also as a `MultiIndex` level or as categories, and copies categories.
