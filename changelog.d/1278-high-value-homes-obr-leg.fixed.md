The high-value homes benchmarks gate reads the vendored OBR costing rows (`uk/obr_hvcts_costing_facts.json`) by concept, England, fiscal year and behaviour basis, so its OBR leg reports Table 1.1 (or 1.2) beside the frame's owner-occupied bands and the derived Land Registry stock. It had looked for an older row shape and reported `awaiting_vendored_facts` on every build (microcosm#1278).

- **The share.** The non-owner-occupied share comes from the note's own row (40 per cent), not from a stock-facts parameter that was never set, so the owner-occupied sensitivity (99,000 for 2028-29, static) and the frame's England total over it are reported. The note states the share once, for 2028-29, and the leg names the year its share is from.
- **The derived stock.** The leg reports the derived stock's private-sector count over Table 1.1's total. The stock build sets the owner-occupied share of England's £2m+ stock from the same OBR share, so an owner-occupied ratio would repeat that one and is not reported.
- **Statuses, not errors.** The leg needs every band and the total of one table, once each, each a finite, non-negative count. Otherwise it reports `no_matching_rows`, `incomplete_rows` or `malformed_rows`, and a share it cannot read leaves the band comparison standing. A missing or non-numeric value, or a row field that is not a mapping, is reported rather than raised, since the leg runs in the terminal phase after the solve.
- **Disclosure.** The shipped band counts now withhold the total when it rests on fewer than ten distinct source households. They withhold further bands until the withheld ones rest together on ten, so the total cannot give their sum back. The regional shares and their total follow the same rule.
- **Informational.** The gate's outcome does not depend on the leg.
- **Cache keys.** The calibrated gate node keys on the high-value homes code through its parameters (`terminal_code`), so a resumed graph store recomputes the evidence when that code changes. The full-gates kernel's own hash, which the source preflight inherits, leaves it out, because the dense solve keys on the preflight. An edit to `high_value_home_gates.py` therefore never re-solves. `high_value_homes.py` also feeds the target kernels, which hash it, so an edit there still re-solves. The hash is taken once per process, so editing the files during a build cannot fail the calibrated gates after the solve.
