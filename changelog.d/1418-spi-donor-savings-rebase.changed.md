The SPI income stage now rebases the 2022-23 tape's interest and other income to HMRC's published 2023-24 Table 3.7 amounts band by band (the published rows are vendored as `uk/hmrc_spi_table_3_7_savings_rows.json`), then carries both to the frame's year on the OBR's savings income growth (October 2026 EFO, Table 4.5), the path the Table 3.7 band targets take (#1418 savings follow-up, #1428). Before, each column moved by its engine index from 2022 to 2024: other income by GDP per head (x1.09) where SPI's own other income grew x1.65 in the single year to 2023-24, and interest by ONS household interest (x2.27) where SPI's grew x2.69 in that year. The frame then carried about 70 percent of the Table 3.7 savings rows at design weights, and the solve met them by stretching the weights of the few households that carry them. Each band's factor is the published amount over the FACT-weighted amount of the tape's taxpayers in the band, as Table 3.7 counts taxpayers only. The tape is banded once on its income moved to 2023-24, each income leaf at its own factor and each rebased column at its factor over all bands, so the band-edge migration its consumers re-band on uprated income is counted once; a record whose band's factor differs from that of all bands can still sit across an edge on its rebased income. A taxpayer is a record the tape flags (TAXPAYER 1) or one whose income crosses the Personal Allowance by 2023-24, so a saver who becomes a taxpayer counts once; a flagged taxpayer below the first band sits in it (note 8), and any other record takes the factor of all bands. A band too thin to gross (under a tenth of its published amount) takes the factor of all bands, a column whose tape does not gross to the published amounts is left as it is and grows on its engine index from 2022-23, and a tape whose TAXPAYER codes are not 1 or 2 is refused. On the tape, the £1m-and-over band's other income outweighs its interest, as in the published table, so the rebase also undoes the split the two indices had flipped.
