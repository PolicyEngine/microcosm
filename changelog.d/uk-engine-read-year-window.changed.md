UK build simulations now hold no year after the one they read. Given a one-year dataset, policyengine-uk (2.177.7) projects it to 2032, and every constructor runs the Universal Credit rebalancing loop over 2026-2032, working out `uc_LCWRA_element` and the arrays behind it for each year; the build reads only 2024 and 2025. Two UK helpers in `microcosm.frame.adapters.policyengine_uk` stop both: `uk_dataset_through` (policyengine-uk's own `extend_single_year_dataset` with `end_year`) and `uk_rebalancing_years_through` (a context manager that narrows `uc_reform.REBALANCING_YEARS` while one simulation is built and restores it after, on success or error). The spine adapter's `materialize` projects its dataset only to the period it reads, on its current-law parameters. The measure resolver in scratch mode, which the dense and national measure steps use, writes its scratch file as a multi-year H5 through its year and builds its main, annual-income and counterfactual simulations inside the loop cut. Release scoring (a direct H5) keeps the whole projection, so policyengine-uk still checks every year of a finished file. Values do not change: a new engine test compares every requested output and every array each simulation and its baseline hold up to the year read, for 2024 data read at 2024 and at 2025 and 2025 data read at 2025, through both routes, on a synthetic frame where the health element is paid. Measured on a synthetic 80,000-household simulation of 2024 data read at 2025, construction falls from 12.1 to 2.1 seconds and peak memory from 5.0 to 3.2 GB. Tests pin the source digests of `add_universal_credit_reform` and `_add_inactive_health_element_formula`, and that `REBALANCING_YEARS` is still a range. policyengine-uk now labels a projected year's CGT inputs "supplied" rather than "projected"; nothing in Microcosm reads that label. A stage that reads its data's own year now projects nothing, and policyengine-uk runs its dataset `validate()` only on a year it projects, so a NaN in a column the engine does not read no longer raises there; a NaN in an engine input still raises, with another message. `uk_dataset_through` refuses an end year before the data's year, so a scratch resolver whose year is before its frame's year now raises `ValueError`; no build does that. `frs_legacy_proxies` is the first UK stage whose implementation hash covers the adapter, and a node key includes its parents' keys, so that stage and every node after it, the dense and national steps included, re-key once; the six FRS stages before it keep their keys.
