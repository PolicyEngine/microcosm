## C11. Vahid's review of `63943a76b` (2026-10-12)

Recorded after #1145 merged, with the wording of its description; nothing here changes code. The control arm on the current base is still owed.

- **Item 1, the HBAI size of the FRS add-back at 2025.** In policyengine-uk 2.123.0 `hbai_household_net_income`, `market_income` and `household_market_income` add `property_income`, profit before finance costs, while HBAI's published basis is FRS property income after show card K6. On the `c9` candidate at its calibrated weights, reversing the add-back (FRS landlords' profit as reported, no finance costs) lowers HBAI household net income by £2.95bn (0.20% of £1,508.5bn) and household market income by £3.29bn, lowers income tax by £0.34bn, cuts the finance-cost relief from £1.77bn to £1.11bn, and raises the before-housing-costs poverty rate by 0.02 points and the after-housing-costs rate by 0.04 points; the Gini of equivalised HBAI income falls by 0.0002. The SPI channel sits on the same before-interest basis. PolicyEngine/policyengine-uk#2390 asks for HBAI and the two market-income variables to read `property_income_after_finance_costs`, as the means tests do: that makes the add-back neutral for HBAI and fixes the SPI side too.
- **Item 2, furnished holiday lettings from 2025-26.** The tape is 2022-23, when FHL landlords still deducted finance costs in full, so their SPI profit is after interest and `LLIR_RESTRICT_AMT_TOT` excludes their costs. The FHL regime ended on 6 April 2025, so at 2025 their interest falls under s. 272A: their `property_income` and `property_finance_costs` are both understated by it. Small, with the direction known.
- **Item 3, capital netting and the finance-cost draw.** FRS mortgage capital stays netted, and it also shifts the draw: the model is fitted on the tape's profit after interest, but FRS landlords are queried with ROYYR1, which is after interest and capital repayments, so a repayment-mortgage landlord takes the incidence and amounts of tape landlords with a lower profit after interest. Most buy-to-let lending is interest-only, so this is small.
